## 1985–1986 — House and Techno

Era: Underground Era
Confidence: clear

- **Early 1980s** — House music emerged from Chicago's Black, Latino and queer dance underground. [Smithsonian: Chicago's queer Black and Latino house underground](https://www.si.edu/object/do-you-remember-house-chicagos-queer-color-undergrounds-micah-salkind%3Asiris_sil_1160644)
- **Mid-1980s** — Techno emerged from Black electronic-music culture in Detroit. [Techno history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techno)

## 1987–1990 — Aciiiiiied !!!

Era: Underground Era
Confidence: clear

- **1987** — The artwork connects early rave foundations and Shuffling to a style it calls UK Rave Stepping. [Rave history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rave) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **1987** — Dropfoot is given as local terminology and Foot Patrol as pioneers. [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **1988** — The Second Summer of Love and Manchester are identified as cultural breakthroughs. [Dance Music Archive: 1988](https://www.dancemusicarchive.com/1988)
- **1988-1990** — Acid House, Breakbeat and Jungle form the musical context. [Acid house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_house) [Breakbeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breakbeat) [Jungle music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jungle_music)
- **Late 1980s** — Leeroy Thornhill, from The Prodigy, is named as a major influence on Melbourne ravers. [Leeroy Thornhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeroy_Thornhill) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **1989** — Running Man becomes a global dance fad associated with Janet Jackson, MC Hammer and Vanilla Ice. [Essence: The Running Man](https://www.essence.com/news/throwback-the-running-man/)

## 1989–1992 — Birth of Melbourne rave scene

Era: Underground Era
Confidence: clear

- **1989** — The dance was called Stomping and is described in the artwork as Pre-Melbourne Shuffle. [Melbourne Shuffle history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_shuffle) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg)
- **1989** — The artwork links this movement to British dance styles, including Northern Soul. [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **1991** — Raves at the Docks warehouse, including Shed 14, helped anchor Melbourne's rave scene. [VICE: birth of Melbourne's rave scene](https://www.vice.com/en/article/we-knew-how-underground-it-was-the-birth-of-melbournes-rave-scene/)
- **1992** — The artwork describes Melbourne's scene as inspired by the UK rave scene. [VICE: birth of Melbourne's rave scene](https://www.vice.com/en/article/we-knew-how-underground-it-was-the-birth-of-melbournes-rave-scene/) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*

- **1995** — Phat pants and hoodies became increasingly visible in the Melbourne rave scene. [Garry Shepherd's Melbourne Shuffle Oldskool archive](https://melbourneshuffleoldskool.blogspot.com/2008/) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **1996-1997** — Street raves became a visible part of the local scene. [VICE: birth of Melbourne's rave scene](https://www.vice.com/en/article/we-knew-how-underground-it-was-the-birth-of-melbournes-rave-scene/) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*

## 1998–2003 — (Oldschool or Original) Melbourne Shuffle

Era: Transition Era
Confidence: supported

- **1998** — The artwork places wider adoption of Melbourne Shuffle as the dance's name in this period. [MSO: how the Melbourne Shuffle name started](https://melbourneshuffleoldskool.blogspot.com/2008/11/rupert-keiller-sonic-animation.html) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
  - Note: Garry Shepherd's account says the name was already in use by 1992.
- **c. 2000** — Rupert Keiller, a member of Sonic Animation, mentioned the name on national television. [MSO: how the Melbourne Shuffle name started](https://melbourneshuffleoldskool.blogspot.com/2008/11/rupert-keiller-sonic-animation.html)
- **c. 2000** — T-Shuffle became a principal move in the oldschool vocabulary. [Melbourne Shuffle history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_shuffle) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **2001** — The scene shifted toward harder EDM styles and Melbourne hard-dance clubs including PHD and Hard Kandy grew. [Garry Shepherd's Melbourne Shuffle Oldskool archive](https://melbourneshuffleoldskool.blogspot.com/2008/) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **2001** — The Docks rave period came to an end. [VICE: birth of Melbourne's rave scene](https://www.vice.com/en/article/we-knew-how-underground-it-was-the-birth-of-melbournes-rave-scene/) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **2002** — The artwork reports an increase in rave drug culture, especially GHB and methamphetamine. [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **c. 2000** — The artwork credits Chris Barry and phatpants.com with consolidating the phat-pants design. [Wayback: phatpants.com](https://web.archive.org/web/*/phatpants.com) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **2001-2002** — Online discussions appeared in forums including inthemix.com.au, melbourneshuffle.com and clept.net. [Wayback: inthemix.com.au](https://web.archive.org/web/*/inthemix.com.au) [Wayback: melbourneshuffle.com](https://web.archive.org/web/*/melbourneshuffle.com)
- **2002** — Club exhibitions and an appearance in The Age are referenced. [Melbourne Shuffle history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_shuffle) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **2003** — Rocking formed as a scene term and style label. [Melbourne Shuffle history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melbourne_shuffle) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **1998-2003** — Phat pants and hoodies became standard Melbourne raver clothing. [Garry Shepherd's Melbourne Shuffle Oldskool archive](https://melbourneshuffleoldskool.blogspot.com/2008/) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*

## 2011–2014 — Cutting Shapes

Era: YouTube & Facebook Era
Confidence: supported

- **2011** — Cutting Shapes began being used in the UK for dance involving shapes made with the whole body. [VICE: Shuffle Wars](https://www.vice.com/da/article/shuffle-wars/)
- **2011** — The artwork contrasts this with Shuffling as a footwork-focused term. [VICE: Shuffle Wars](https://www.vice.com/da/article/shuffle-wars/) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **2011-2013** — The original London scene was led substantially by Black dancers. [VICE: Shuffle Wars](https://www.vice.com/da/article/shuffle-wars/) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg)
- **2012** — The #IGOTSHAPES YouTube series documented the scene. [The Guardian: London's deep-tech and shuffling scene](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/dec/30/how-deep-tech-became-clubbings-biggest-success-story) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **2012** — Names including Shapeshifting, UK Shuffle, House Shuffle and London Shuffle circulated. [VICE: Shuffle Wars](https://www.vice.com/da/article/shuffle-wars/) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **2013** — Club restrictions and opposition to the dance became known as the Shuffle Wars. [VICE: Shuffle Wars](https://www.vice.com/da/article/shuffle-wars/)
- **2013** — VICE released a documentary report with testimony and footage from the scene. [VICE: Shuffle Wars](https://www.vice.com/da/article/shuffle-wars/)
- **2014** — The original UK Cutting Shapes scene began to fade. [The Guardian: London's deep-tech and shuffling scene](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2014/dec/30/how-deep-tech-became-clubbings-biggest-success-story) [Original STL artwork](https://jumpstyle.com.br/STL/assets/source/shuffle-timeline-8k.jpg) *(needs review)*
- **2017** — Fortnite adapted Gabby J David's choreography as the Electro Shuffle emote without crediting her. [VICE: Fortnite and Gabby J David's choreography](https://www.vice.com/en/article/fornite-suit-dance-moves-black-artists/) [Gabby J David: Electro Shuffle tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Orb4pawcfFY)
